# Huddle

A native, lightweight Matrix client for macOS. It is built with SwiftUI on the [Matrix Rust SDK](https://github.com/matrix-org/matrix-rust-sdk), the same engine as Element X, so it uses native sliding sync and end-to-end encryption. Several accounts can be signed in at once, all syncing side by side.

- **Native:** SwiftUI, no Electron or web view. The app is about 50 MB and idles around 65 MB of memory.
- **Sliding sync:** the room list loads instantly whatever the account's size. The rooms on screen in the list are kept subscribed, so their previews (reactions included) stay current and opening one shows its latest messages at once; rooms further down get only their newest event until they scroll into view. Homeservers without native sliding sync (MSC4186) are not supported.
- **Multiple accounts:** small avatars in the title bar above the sidebar switch accounts (⌃1–9), with any that don't fit in a +N menu; one pointed at shows a faint circle, the chip it would open into. The open account opens into a chip with its name, which shows the account's menu when clicked, as right-clicking any account's avatar does: the same items as its ⋯ menu in Settings ▸ Accounts (Copy User ID, Label, Edit Profile, Change Password or Manage Account, Devices, Verify and the recovery key where there's something to do, Sign Out), with Mark All as Read, New Space and Add Account as well; each has its own unread badge and its own encrypted local store. Settings ▸ Accounts lists them with whether this Mac is verified for each, and has Verify (emoji or recovery key), Set Up or Change Recovery Key, Copy User ID, Label (a name of your own for the account, shown beside it, in the title bar's tooltips and in notifications), Sign Out and Add Account.
- **Spaces:** an account's spaces sit in a rail down the left of the sidebar (shown once it has a space or an invitation to one), each with a badge for its rooms that need attention. The one shown has a ring around it in the text colour (white in dark mode, black in light), which stands out whatever a space's picture, and one pointed at has the same ring, faint; a click shows in the rail at once, and the room list and the conversation follow as soon as each is drawn. Choosing one narrows the room list to its rooms, including those in the spaces within it. Home, the house at the top of the rail, holds the direct chats and rooms that aren't in any space, and invitations to them; a chat or room filed in a space is in that space only, so it lights up one badge. Searching the room list finds rooms in the list showing first, then any found elsewhere, under Elsewhere; choosing one of those opens it and takes the list to its space (or Home). Browsing a space lists every room and space in it, joined or not, to join or go into, and an invitation to a space shows who sent it and what is inside before you accept. Make a space (the + at the foot of the rail), or a room (File ▸ New Room…, ⌥⌘N) that its members, only people invited, or anyone can join; add rooms, direct chats and spaces you're in to a space (Add Existing Rooms…) or take them out again (Remove from… in a room's menu in the list, or right-clicking anything while browsing the space, where spaces within it are too); and leave a space along with any of its rooms. A space's name, topic and picture are changed on its page, where the space lets you (Edit Name and Topic…, or Edit Space… in its menu; click the picture to change or remove it); the rail, the list and other members' apps show the change. A direct chat filed in a space is only listed by the space: nothing is written into the chat, so the people in it aren't told which space it's in, and the space's other members can't see it there. A space added to another goes inside it: it leaves the rail, its rooms show in the outer space's list, and browsing the outer space opens it in place; taken out again, it's back in the rail.
- **Sections:** the room list is in sections that fold away by clicking their header (a folded one shows a badge for what needs attention): Invitations and Favourites, then People, your own sections and Rooms in the order you set, and Low Priority last. They are Element's sections (2026), shared both ways with Element on any device. A room's menu has Favourite and Low Priority ticks, as Element's does (and Room ▸ Favourite and Low Priority, for the open room), and moves it with Move To to any section, a new one included; rooms can also be dragged onto a section. A Low Priority room with only new messages (no count, mention or marking unread) doesn't light up its space's badge in the rail, as Element leaves it out, and Next Unread Room passes over it; its own row still shows them. File ▸ New Section…, a section header's menu, or a space's own menu (its ⋯ button, or right-clicking it in the rail) makes a section (just above Rooms, as Element does); one made in a space is kept there. Headers move People, Rooms and your sections up or down, and your own sections' headers rename or delete them (their rooms go back to People and Rooms). Stored as Element stores them: Favourites and Low Priority are the standard Matrix tags, a room in one of your sections carries its `element.io.section.<id>` tag (one section per room), and the sections' names and order are Element's `RoomList.CustomSectionData` and `RoomList.OrderedCustomSections` settings in your account (`im.vector.web.settings`, whose other settings are left alone). Huddle reads every room's tags with a sliding sync connection of its own that asks only for account data, then checks for changes every 30 seconds; sections made in Huddle before it matched Element are moved over once. Within a space, the sections hold that space's rooms; searching unfolds them all.
- **Devices:** Devices… (in an account's ⋯ menu in Settings ▸ Accounts, or its avatar's menu in the title bar) lists every device signed in to the account: this Mac first, then the others, most recently active first, each with when and from where it was last active, whether it's verified (signed by your cross-signing key, the signatures checked on this Mac against the key it holds, not taken on the server's word; shown once this Mac is verified itself), or without encryption at all, and devices unused for 90 days marked inactive. Devices can be renamed, and signed out one at a time or all at once, confirmed with your password. Where the server hands sign-in to its own service (Matrix Authentication Service), signing out opens that service's page for the device instead.
- **Settings (⌘,):** tabs for Notifications, Appearance (message layout and emoji size, with a live preview of a short conversation, and the quick reactions), Privacy, Accounts and Updates.
- **Your profile and password:** Edit Profile… (in an account's ⋯ menu in Settings ▸ Accounts, or its avatar's menu in the title bar) changes your display name and picture, cropped square, shown first and saved with Save. Change Password… asks for the current password and the new one twice, and can sign out your other devices (this Mac stays signed in). Accounts whose server hands sign-in to its own service (Matrix Authentication Service) change their password on that service's account page: Manage Account… opens it, as does Change Password… where the server doesn't let apps change passwords.
- **Updates:** Huddle ▸ Check for Updates…, or Check Now in Settings ▸ Updates, looks for a newer release; it also checks once a day by itself unless that's turned off. A new version shows what changed, then downloads, is checked against Huddle's signing key before it is even unpacked, installs and relaunches ([Sparkle](https://sparkle-project.org)). Settings can also have updates installed without asking (when Huddle quits) and offer beta versions. The Dev build never updates itself.
- **Sign-in:** username and password in the app wherever the homeserver accepts them, with OAuth in your browser as the alternative (needed for single sign-on, passkeys and two-step verification) or where it is the only way. Only over HTTPS (plain HTTP just for a server on this Mac, as in testing), including where the server's address leads to another.
- **Chat:** searchable room list with previews and unread badges (a faint wash on the row pointed at), invites, a timeline with history paging, images, reactions with a searchable emoji picker, formatted messages with mention pills, and a markdown composer that suggests people as you type "@", and emoji as you type ":" and a name (":thu" offers 👍; Return or Tab picks one), as in Slack and Element; a shortcode closed with its colon, such as ":tada:" or ":fire:", becomes its emoji as you type it. Names are matched from their words' starts and as usually typed ("smile" finds the smiling faces), and the shortcodes people know from Slack and GitHub work too, in the reaction picker's search as well. The reaction picker is the same wherever it opens, from a message's hover actions or beside its reactions: your quick reactions on top, and those you've already reacted with marked, as picking one again takes it back. People's names, and avatars without a picture, have Element's colours: Compound's six, picked from the user ID as Element and Element X pick them, so someone looks the same in Huddle as in Element (a direct chat's avatar has the other person's). In a room of up to six people nobody shares one: where Element's would give two people the same, the one whose user ID sorts later takes a colour nobody in the room has. Settings (⌘,) switches between three message layouts: modern, bubbles and a compact IRC-style one. Bubbles puts your messages on the right in a pale wash of the accent colour and others' on the left, a run of one person's messages hugging together with a tail on the last; in rooms of more than two, their avatar sits by their last bubble and their name over the first; the time shows where the conversation picks up after a pause, and on hover over any bubble. Pictures, files and big emoji stand on their own. Hovering a bubble shows three small buttons beside it: react (a picker with the quick reactions along its top), reply, and more. The quick reactions are six of your choosing (Settings ▸ Appearance; click one to put another in its place, or right-click one on a message and choose Customize Quick Reactions…): all six top the reaction picker in every layout, and the first three, or all six if you'd rather (On a message's bar), sit on a message's bar in the modern and IRC layouts and in threads. Where six won't fit, as in a thread beside the conversation, the bar shows three. In the IRC layout, drag the edge between the names and the messages to resize the names column, or double-click it to fit the longest name; each room remembers its own width.
- **Images:** pictures show at their own size up to a sensible limit, with the sender's blurred preview while they load; GIFs play in place. A picture is downloaded without being clicked only up to 20 MB, and drawn only up to 64 megapixels; a larger one (in an encrypted room, say, sent without a smaller copy) shows Show Picture with its size until clicked. The size that counts is the server's, not the one in the message, which its sender can set to anything. One of more than 256 megapixels isn't shown at all, in the conversation or full size (that could take gigabytes): it says so, and can be saved to open in another app. One sent without its size (as some clients send pictures) takes its true shape once it loads; above what you're reading, that stays where it is, as it does when older messages are drawn above it while you scroll up, mid-scroll too. Clicking one opens it full size over the window, where it can be zoomed, rotated, moved, copied and saved: a click on the picture zooms in on that spot at once (to its own size, or twice the size shown), and another goes back to fit; a click outside it, or Escape, closes it. Send images with the + button, by pasting, or by dragging them in: they wait in the composer with a caption, upload one at a time in order with progress, and can be cancelled or retried. HEIC and TIFF are converted so every client can show them.
- **Files:** send any file from the + button, by dropping it on the conversation, or by pasting it after copying it in the Finder. Files wait in the composer as tiles with their icon, name and size (the text typed goes with them as a caption), and upload one at a time, read from disk as they go, with how far each has got and a button to stop it. Videos and recordings go as video and audio messages, so other apps can play them; a video goes with a still from its opening and a blur of it, which Element and other apps show until it's played. **Videos** play in the conversation: each is drawn at its own shape, as a picture is, with a still from it (the sender's, or one Huddle takes from the film when the sender sent none and the server says it's under 20 MB), a play button and its length. Click to play it in place, with macOS's own controls on hover (pause, scrubber, times and full screen); it downloads, and decrypts, only then, and is kept on disk while Huddle runs, so playing it again is instant. One plays at a time: starting another, or an audio message, stops it, as leaving the room does. An upright phone video sits in a frame wide enough for the controls, black either side, as QuickTime shows it. A video macOS can't play (WebM, Matroska) is a file tile, and one that turns out not to play says so and opens in another app when clicked. Its menu, on the still or while it plays, opens it in its usual app, naming it ("Open in QuickTime Player"), copies it to paste in Finder, Mail or another conversation (Copy Video), or saves it (Save Video As…). In the conversation any other file is a tile with its icon, name, size and kind: click it to download and open it in its usual app (its menu names the app, as "Open in Preview"), or choose Save File As… from its menu. Files that are downloaded are marked as downloaded, so macOS checks them before anything opens them. A file opened from Huddle is a copy, decrypted, that goes an hour later or when Huddle quits, whichever is first (one you've changed in another app stays a day, so your changes aren't lost); Save File As… keeps one. Videos played are decrypted to a folder of Huddle's own, emptied when it quits.
- **Media and files:** Room Info ▸ Media and Files (or Room ▸ Media and Files) shows everything sent in a room as a picture, video, sound or file, thread replies included and encrypted rooms too, one kind at a time: Images, Videos, Audio, PDFs and Files (every file, PDFs among them), newest first under the month they were sent, going back through the room's history as you scroll, with how many there are once it reaches the start. What's already on this Mac goes by in moments; from the server, a long conversation can take minutes, so once nothing of the kind has turned up for ten seconds it stops, saying how many it found in the latest messages, with Look Further Back to go on. Pictures and videos are a grid of thumbnails, each video with its length; sounds play where they are and files open in their app, as in the conversation. A picture opens full size; a video goes to its message, where it plays. Each one's menu goes to its message in the conversation (or opens its thread) and saves it.
- **Replies:** reply to any message from its hover actions or context menu. A reply shows the message it answers above it, and clicking that jumps to the original, loading older history if needed. Works inside threads too.
- **Editing and deleting:** edit your messages, or a picture's caption, from the ⋯ button in a message's hover actions or its context menu; the message goes into the composer with its mentions intact, and whatever you were writing is set aside until you save (Return) or cancel (Escape). Up arrow in an empty composer edits your last message. Delete asks first; room moderators can remove other people's messages.
- **Pinning:** pin messages for everyone in the room (where the room allows it). A banner at the top of the room shows the pinned messages one at a time: clicking it goes to that message, its arrows show the others in turn without going there, its pin or View All lists them all in the side panel, and its × puts it away in that room (Room ▸ Show Pinned Banner brings it back). The list, also in Room Info ▸ Pinned Messages (with how many there are) and Room ▸ Pinned Messages (⇧⌘P), shows each pinned message in full, as the conversation does: its text to read and select, links working, a picture to open full size, a file to open, a sound or video to play, with who sent it, the day and time, and whether it was in a thread. Pointed at, each has Go to Message (Show in Thread for one in a thread) and Unpin; its menu has those and Copy Text. A message from far back (a pin, or the original of a reply) opens in a view of its own around it, without loading the whole room's history up to it; Jump to Latest goes back.
- **Forwarding:** send any message, picture or file on to other rooms from the ⋯ menu: search for a room and press Send beside each one it should go to.
- **Direct and group chats:** File ▸ New Message… (⌘N), or the compose button above the room list, opens a To field. Find people by name or Matrix ID (the people already chatted with first, then your server's directory) and add them with a click or Return; Backspace takes the last one off. Going (the button, or Return in the empty field) takes you to the chat you already have with them: the direct chat with one person, or a group of exactly those people (with a way to start a new one anyway). Otherwise it starts one, unencrypted unless you tick End-to-end encryption; a group can be given a name. A full Matrix ID can always be added; one the server says nobody has is shown as such rather than invited.
- **Room info:** the ⓘ button on a room (Room ▸ Room Info, ⌘I) opens its details beside the conversation: picture, name and topic, whether it's encrypted, and who is in it, with their roles and who is only invited. A room's topic also shows under its name in the window's title, as Slack and Element show one: its first line, cut short where the window ends; a direct chat has the person's whereabouts there instead. A change of topic says what it now is ("Alice changed the topic to …"), and browsing a space shows each room's topic under its name. Clicking the picture shows it large, in the same viewer as pictures in messages: in a direct chat, the person's own (unless the chat has a picture of its own). Where your power level allows, the click offers View Picture, choosing a new one (cropped square) or removing it instead; you can also edit the name and topic, and turn encryption on (after a warning that it can't be turned off). Invite… beside the member count opens the same To field as New Message, with those already in the room shown but not choosable. The ⋯ button that appears on hover (or the right-click menu) has a Role submenu, for Admin, Moderator or Member, where your power level lets you change roles: anyone below you can be given any role up to your own, and you can lower your own. The two changes you can't take back (giving someone your own level, or lowering yourself) ask first; an owner's role can't be changed. Role changes show in the conversation for everyone, as "Alice made Bob a moderator" or "Bob stepped down as admin". Admins and moderators can also take someone below them out of the room, or withdraw an invitation, after a confirmation. Leave the room from there too.
- **Your names for people:** give anyone a name of your own, as a phone keeps contacts ("Mum", "Sam from work"): Set Name… in a direct chat's Room Info, or in a member's menu there, edits it where their name is shown: in the chat's header, as a room's name is edited, or in place in the member's row, as Finder renames a file (Return keeps it, Escape doesn't). Left empty, they go by their own name again. Huddle then shows it wherever it names them for that account: their messages and replies, their direct chat in the room list and its title, a room with no name of its own that's called after its people ("Sam, Carol"), the room list's previews, typing, read receipts, reactions, search, calls and notifications. Room Info keeps their own name beneath, and typing "@" finds them by either. A mention reads by your name for them too, in the message field, in pills, quotes and previews; only you see it, though: the message sends their own name, which is what the room reads. The names are kept in your account's data on the server, so Huddle on your other Macs has them too;
- **Search:** Edit ▸ Find (⌘F), or the magnifying glass on a room, opens a search beside the conversation, in this room or all of them (⇧⌘F). Results show who wrote what and when, with the words found highlighted (other forms of them too); clicking one goes to the message, in its own room if need be (an edit to the message it changed, a reply in a thread to the thread), and the arrow keys, ⌘G and ⇧⌘G go through them in turn. Words match from their start, as you type ("hel" finds "hello"), and a message has to have all of them; this Mac's index (patched for it, in `Scripts/sdk-patches`) reads the search as plain words, so punctuation in it ("Re:", quotes) never breaks it. Two searches run together and are merged, newest first: the homeserver's own, which covers the whole history of unencrypted rooms with nothing downloaded, and an index on this Mac, kept by the Matrix SDK and encrypted with the account's store key, which covers encrypted rooms. New messages go into it as they arrive; a pass in the background then goes through every encrypted room back to its first message (and through unencrypted rooms too, on a server that can't search), remembering which rooms are done. That pass, and the SDK's own first pass over every room's last three months, run only while you aren't using Huddle: while it's in the background, or after a minute untouched. Typing, clicking or scrolling in it pauses them at once, so they never compete with what you're doing. A room you have open (or had open recently) waits until it isn't, in this pass and in the SDK's own first pass over every room's last three months (patched to, in `Scripts/sdk-patches`), and one in view in the room list has its reactions check paused meanwhile: the SDK shares a room's loaded history with every timeline on it, so neither would otherwise grow to the room's whole history, redrawing all the way. Older messages that arrive above the conversation all the same, fetched for something else, aren't drawn until you scroll up to them. The search panel and Settings ▸ Privacy show its progress, and Settings can turn it off. Old encrypted messages are found once their keys are available, which needs this Mac verified with the recovery key.
- **Read and unread:** mark a room read or unread from its context menu, or the selected room with Room ▸ Mark as Read/Unread (⇧⌘U); Mark All as Read is in the same menus and on an account's avatar. A room marked unread gets a blue dot (other unread rooms a grey one) and stays unread, even the one that is open, until it is opened again or you write in it. While the open room is marked unread, a bar along its top says so (your read receipts are paused meanwhile) and has a Mark as Read button. Otherwise a room is read when you open it at its newest messages (its threads apart: see Threads), and the open room as new messages arrive at its end, only while it is in front of you: messages that come while Huddle is in the background, another of its windows (Settings) is in front, its window is out of sight (the Mac locked or its display asleep, on another desktop or covered by other windows), a sheet is over the room or a picture is open stay unread until you're back, and are read then if the room is at its newest messages.
- **Large emoji:** a message of nothing but a few emoji (up to 12) is drawn large. Settings ▸ Appearance chooses Normal, Large or Extra Large.
- **Audio:** voice messages and audio files play in the timeline, with a waveform or track to scrub through, 1×/1.5×/2× speed, and Save As. Ogg Opus, which macOS can't play by itself, is decoded in the app, up to 45 minutes (22 in stereo). **Recording:** with nothing typed, the send button is a microphone (or Room ▸ Record Voice Message, ⇧⌘R). The message field shows a red dot, the time and the sound as it comes in; stop it to listen before sending, or send it straight away (Return, or the send button). The trash button deletes it; so does Escape, which asks first once a recording is five seconds long. A voice message goes as Element sends one, Ogg Opus at 32 kbit/s with its waveform, encoded as it's recorded by macOS's own Opus encoder, and can be up to 15 minutes. It goes as a reply if one is set up, and in a thread from a thread's composer. Leaving the room stops a recording without losing it: it waits in the composer, and the room list says "Draft: Voice message" meanwhile. Starting a recording stops audio or video playing.
- **Threads:** Slack-style. Replies stay out of the room; the root message shows a reply count that opens the thread in a side panel with its own composer, beside the conversation when the window has room and over it when it hasn't. The panel slides in from the window's edge, and moving between the thread list and a thread slides like a page turn; with Reduce Motion on, these become fades. Drag the panel's edge to resize it (double-click for the standard width). Threads use the room's layout, except IRC, which is too cramped for the panel: there they use the modern one. A toolbar button lists all of a room's threads. A reply from someone else to a thread you haven't read is easy to find however far back the thread started: the room list says "Bob in a thread: …" when it's the room's newest message, and in the room a pill over the conversation says "Bob replied in a thread" (or how many threads have new replies) and opens it (or the list); the Threads button has a dot, and the list and the root message mark it New replies. A thread is read when you read it, in its panel or by answering its alert (or the whole room with Mark as Read), not by reading the rest of the conversation: a room whose only unread messages are in threads stays unread, its row showing a threads badge in place of a count (grey in a room that tells only of mentions), and keeps those threads' alerts. This goes by your read receipts in each thread, as the server and Element count them, so it holds after quitting and across your devices; the SDK's own counts take a room as one line, and would take a thread's replies as read once a newer message is. Huddle looks up the newest reply's own date for each thread, as the SDK's thread list gives it the root's, and ignores older replies the SDK takes for newer ones.
- **Presence:** direct chats show whether the other person is online (green) or away (amber) on their avatar, and "Online" or "Last seen 5 min ago" under the room's name. Huddle tells your server you're online while it's in front and away a couple of minutes after; Settings can turn sharing off, which, as in most messaging apps, also hides everyone else's. Servers that don't do presence simply show nothing.
- **Typing:** start typing anywhere in the window and it goes to the composer, as does pasting (⌘V, pictures and files too); the arrow keys still move through the room list. A room clicked in the list leaves the keyboard there, as a Mac sidebar does, so its row stays highlighted and the arrow keys carry on from it; one opened any other way is ready to type in. Return sends; Shift-Return (or Option-Return) starts a new line, and the composer grows to eight lines before it scrolls. Line breaks go out as Element sends them. What you're writing stays with its room (or thread) when you go elsewhere and is there when you come back, with the message it replies to and any pictures or files; the text is kept across launches too, encrypted with the account's store, as the messages themselves are. Meanwhile the room list shows it in place of the room's newest message ("Draft: …" with "Draft" in red, or "Draft in thread: …", or "Draft: 2 images" when only pictures or files are waiting), unless something new has come there since you last read it, which shows instead until you have.
- **Typing indicators:** while others write, the end of the conversation says so ("Alice is typing", "Alice and Bob are typing"), with three pulsing dots where an avatar would be, and the room list says so in place of the room's newest message, in colour ("typing…" in a direct chat, "Alice is typing…" in a room; not in muted rooms). Others see when you're typing, from your first keystroke until you send, clear the message, or pause for a few seconds; Settings can turn sharing off, which also hides when others are typing. The switch is Element's "Send typing notifications", saved with each account on the server, so Element, Huddle on your other Macs and this one all follow it. A room opened mid-message shows who's typing straight away: Huddle follows typing in every room, since the server only says when it changes.
- **Calls:** voice calls with Element Call, as Element X and Element Web make them (MatrixRTC), so you can call people who use those, and they you. The phone button on a room calls the other person in a direct chat, ringing them, or starts or joins a voice call in a room; in the call, it becomes End Call. A call shows at the foot of the room list, wherever you are in Huddle: who it's with (clicking it goes to the room), how long it has run, or how many are in a room's call, with Mute and End Call. Element Call runs out of sight inside Huddle, which works its mute and hang-up; closing Huddle's window keeps the call going, and quitting leaves it. Someone calling you shows a panel at the top right of the screen with Answer and Decline and rings, with macOS's own "Opening" ringtone (the iPhone's, which FaceTime also offers) over and over, until you answer or decline, they give up, or you answer on another device (with no alert as well); a call that rings out or that they give up on leaves a missed-call alert in Notification Center. Rooms with a call going on show a green phone in the list. In the conversation, a call's line says how it went: "You called · No answer", "You called · Cancelled", "Bob called · Missed", "Bob called · 1 min, 23 sec" or "· Declined" for the older kind (from its answer, decline and end, which the SDK is patched to pass on), and "· Declined", "· In progress" or "· Ended" for Element Call's. Calls are end-to-end encrypted where the room is. They need your server to support Element Call (a LiveKit call server), as calls in Element X do; a room whose settings don't let members take part in calls has the button greyed, and the rooms and chats Huddle makes allow calls, as Element's do. **Without Element Call:** where your server doesn't run it, or the room doesn't allow it, a call in a chat of two people is the older kind of Matrix one-to-one call, the kind Element Web makes in a direct chat, which needs nothing more of the server than messages do. It shows at the foot of the room list in the same way, with a crossed-out microphone by the other person's name when they've muted. While it rings the other person, you hear it ringing (a ringback tone, two seconds in every six, as phones in North America ring back), and the room list says "You’re calling", as the conversation does ("Bob is calling", or "Calling you" in a direct chat, for one ringing in); calls of this kind ringing in, from Element Web or Huddle, show the same panel with Answer and Decline. The two Macs connect directly: through your server's TURN server if it has one, and otherwise finding the way with matrix.org's STUN server, the fallback Element offers, which sees your address while a call connects. Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ Use matrix.org to help calls connect turns that off; such a call then connects only where the two Macs can reach each other directly. Element X makes only Element Call's calls. Huddle asks for the microphone the first time; there is no camera. Element Call is bundled in Huddle (`Scripts/fetch-element-call.sh`) and licensed under the AGPL-3.0, its licence and source in the app's Licenses folder. Its page runs only its own code: a content security policy, added as Huddle is built (`Scripts/element-call-csp.py`), lets it load scripts from the app alone, with no frames or plugins, and connect only over HTTPS (or to a server on this Mac); and only that page, in the call's own frame, can talk to Huddle and through it to the room. The older kind of call's page loads nothing at all.
- **Read receipts:** in direct chats, ticks on your messages (one grey tick while sending, two grey once sent, two coloured once read); in group rooms, small avatars beside the last message each person has read, with the full list on click.
- **Notifications:** macOS alerts for new messages, mentions and invitations, following your account's notification rules (muted rooms stay silent; direct messages and mentions play a sound). With more than one account, each alert says which it arrived on: by the label you've given it ("Bob · on Work"; Label… in its ⋯ menu in Settings ▸ Accounts, kept in your account's data on the server, so Huddle on your other Macs has it too), otherwise by server ("Bob · on acme.com"), or by full Matrix ID when two of the accounts share a server; missed calls and verification requests too. Clicking one opens that account, room and thread, with the room list on the room's space if the space showing doesn't hold it, and reading a room clears its alerts (a thread's, once the thread is read). A message's alert has a Reply field, as in Messages: what's typed there goes into the room as a message of its own (into the thread, for a message in one), written as in the message field (Markdown), and the room counts as read, as it has been answered. Replying to an alert left from before Huddle quit starts it, and the reply goes once the account is back; one that can't be sent says so in an alert, and the words wait in the room's message field. Clicking an alert while Huddle is closed opens the room the same way. The room you have open gets no alert while you're in Huddle, even with Settings in front or a picture open, unless Huddle's window is out of sight: in the Dock, on another desktop or covered by other windows. Each room can notify for all messages, only for mentions and keywords, or not at all (muted), or follow your account's default: choose from Notifications in its right-click menu or in Room Info (⌘I). The choice is kept in your push rules, so Element and other apps share it; muted rooms show a crossed-out bell in the list, and their new messages don't mark them unread. Settings (⌘,) turns alerts or their sound off; they start off in the Dev build. It can also leave the text out of them (Show message text): an alert then says who wrote and where, but not what, as macOS keeps alerts in a database of its own, outside Huddle's encryption.
- **Encryption:** end-to-end encrypted rooms work. A small lock at the start of the message field says whether what you type will be encrypted: a faint closed lock if so, an open one if not (amber in direct chats and private rooms, where encryption is expected; grey in public rooms, where it usually isn't). Clicking the lock opens Room Info. A new Mac is verified from the "This Mac isn't verified" banner under the room list (or Settings ▸ Accounts): by comparing seven emoji with one of your other devices (Huddle, Element or any Matrix app), or with your recovery key. Requests from elsewhere come to Huddle too: a new device of yours asking to be verified, or someone asking to verify with you, opens a sheet with the device's name, ID and when it signed in, to accept or decline; if another sheet is open, a banner under the room list waits instead, and a notification announces it when Huddle isn't in front. Emoji that don't match stop it, and the other side is told why. **Recovery key:** a verified Mac without one is offered one (a dismissible "No recovery key yet" under the room list, and Settings ▸ Accounts), shown once in large type to copy (marked so clipboard managers skip it) or save to a file only you can read, and the sheet can't be finished until it's kept. Change Recovery Key… in an account's ⋯ menu replaces it, the old one stopping working. An account whose key backup this Mac can't open (one left by a device since reset) can replace the old backup, after a warning. **Other people:** Verify… in a member's menu in Room Info asks them to compare emoji too (the request goes in your direct chat with them, made if there isn't one), and people you've verified carry a green seal there (an orange or red badge if their identity changed). A direct chat's Room Info says where the other person stands, under Encryption: verified, not verified (with Verify…), identity reset (with Verify… and Accept, or Withdraw Verification if you had verified them), or unable to be verified. Huddle asks the server about anyone it doesn't keep track of, so this holds in unencrypted chats too; answering a change here or in the bar above the composer clears both at once. When someone's identity changes, a bar above the composer says so (orange, with OK to accept the new identity; red if you had verified them, with Withdraw Verification), and a message sent from a device its owner hasn't verified, an unknown device, or someone else's, says so underneath, with what that means on hover. Your own messages carry only the serious of these: one sent unencrypted into an encrypted room (which Huddle never does, but a server could pass off as yours), or from an unknown device, or once this Mac is verified, from one of your devices you haven't verified. Requests to verify show in the chat as a short line ("You asked to verify"), not their fallback text. **Exclude unverified devices** (Settings ▸ Privacy, off by default; Element calls it "exclude insecure devices"): encrypted messages you send are shared only with devices their owners have verified, messages from other devices are hidden with the reason shown ("Hidden: sent from a device its owner hasn't verified"), and this Mac must be verified to send. It applies when Huddle starts, with Restart Now beside it after a change. Messages that can't be decrypted say why when the SDK knows ("Sent before you joined", "Not shared with this Mac, which isn't verified"), and a message that couldn't be sent says why after "Not sent". **Lost everything?** "Can't confirm?" on the Verify sheet resets your identity: it says what's lost, confirms with your password (or, on a server with its own sign-in service such as Matrix Authentication Service, approval on its page, which Huddle opens and waits for), makes this Mac the verified start of a new identity, and goes straight on to a new recovery key.
- **Keyboard:** the Go menu moves around, as Element's keys do, and works while you're typing a message (not while a sheet or a picture is open over it): Go to Room… (⌘K) finds any room by name across all accounts and spaces (before anything is typed, the rooms visited lately), the arrow keys choose and Return goes there; Back and Forward (⌘[, ⌘]) go through the rooms visited, across accounts and spaces, as a browser does; Next Room and Previous Room (⌥↓, ⌥↑) go through the rooms as the list shows them, and Next Unread Room and Previous Unread Room (⌥⌘↓, ⌥⌘↑) skip to the ones with something new, folded sections included; Home and the account's spaces, in the rail's order, are ⌘1 to ⌘9 (said in each one's tooltip); Next Account and Previous Account are ⌃Tab and ⌃⇧Tab (or ⌥⌘→ and ⌥⌘←: with two accounts, back and forth), and the accounts themselves ⌃1 to ⌃9. View ▸ Show/Hide Sidebar (⌃⌘S). Window ▸ Huddle (⌘0) brings the window back after it has been closed.
- **Picking up where you left off:** the window comes back as it was left, and each account reopens the room it last had open (in the space showing). A room you leave scrolled up through its history opens just there when you come back to it, while Huddle stays open: the same message at the same height, with the arrow down to the newest messages in the corner. If a long stretch of its history was loaded, it opens in a view of its own around that message, as a search result does, with Jump to Latest; if that can't be loaded (offline, say), at its newest messages. A notification, a search result or starting a new message opens the room at what it's about instead. The window is kept under a fixed name, so an update can't stop macOS finding it again; if no window comes back at launch all the same, Huddle opens one.
- **Safety:** links in messages open only web pages and email; any other kind is refused, saying what it was, and a link whose text names a different address from where it goes (anywhere in it, invisible characters or not) asks first. A mention pill always names the person it points to, whatever the sender wrote. Names people give themselves, their rooms, spaces and devices are shown cleaned of what could make them read as something else: characters that turn text round or hide in it, accents stacked into towers, names with nothing visible (shown as the Matrix ID instead). Two people in a room going by the same name, or names that look the same, show their Matrix IDs as well, as in Element (in notifications, once the room's members are known on this Mac). Formatting nested to absurd depths, and images or voice messages that would unpack to gigabytes, are cut short rather than stall the app. Before a picture, thumbnail, video or GIF is downloaded unasked, the server is asked its size (a HEAD request), as the size in a message can be anything; only a server that won't say leaves the message's word to go by. Saved and opened files are marked as downloaded, so macOS checks them before opening (a file that can't be marked isn't kept), and their names can't hide a different kind of file. Opening a kind of file that can run something (an app, a script, an installer or disk image, a configuration profile, a web shortcut) asks first. A call rings for at most two minutes, whatever the caller sets, and the older kind of call rings only in a room of two. The app is signed with the hardened runtime, so no other process can inject code into it; the release workflow won't publish an update that isn't.

## Requirements

- A Mac running macOS 13 or later, with Apple Silicon or an Intel processor: the release app is one app for both.

This is the `legacy/macos-13` branch: Huddle for macOS 13 (Ventura), as 0.13.x, made from 1.0. Huddle 1.0 and later need macOS 14; this line keeps Macs that can't go past 13 (2017 models) going. It differs from main where macOS 13 lacks something: Point-Free's Perception stands in for Apple's Observation (views that read the app's state wrap their content in `WithPerceptionTracking`, which is how macOS 13 notices what they read), and `Sources/Huddle/Views/Compat.swift` has a macOS 13 stand-in for each newer SwiftUI piece, SwiftUI's own on macOS 14. Dev builds print `PERCEPTION_UNTRACKED` for any state a view reads without tracking it, on any macOS: a view macOS 13 wouldn't redraw. The release workflow lists each release for macOS 13 only, so Sparkle offers this line to macOS 13 and the newer one to macOS 14 and later; main's build numbers count from 1000, above any of this line's, because Sparkle goes by build number, not version.
- Xcode with Swift 5.10 or later (a release build, for both kinds of Mac at once, needs Xcode itself, not only its command line tools).
- Rust, only to compile the Matrix SDK, with both Mac targets: `brew install rustup && rustup default stable && rustup target add aarch64-apple-darwin x86_64-apple-darwin`.

## Building

Element publishes the Matrix SDK prebuilt for iOS only, so the first step compiles it for macOS, for Apple Silicon and Intel in one library. This takes 20–40 minutes and is needed once, and again whenever the SDK commit pinned in `Scripts/build-sdk.sh` or a patch in `Scripts/sdk-patches` changes. Its output lands in `Vendor/MatrixRustSDK` and is not kept in git.

`Scripts/sdk-patches` holds fixes to the SDK's dependencies, applied as the SDK is built. `ruma-html-deep-nesting.patch` frees a message's parsed HTML one node at a time: freed recursively, as ruma does, HTML nested a few thousand levels deep (which fits in one message) overflows the stack and crashes the app, at every launch while that message is in a room. When the SDK commit is bumped, check whether ruma has fixed this itself, and drop the patch if so.

```bash
Scripts/build-sdk.sh
```

Then build the app:

```bash
Scripts/build-app.sh          # release → build/Huddle.app
Scripts/build-app.sh debug    # debug   → build/Huddle Dev.app
open build/Huddle.app
```

Add accounts from the sign-in screen, an account's right-click menu in the title bar, or **File → Add Account…** (⇧⌘N). To sign out, right-click an account's avatar.

### Keychain prompts after rebuilding

Sessions are stored in the Keychain, and macOS ties Keychain access to an app's Team ID. `Scripts/build-app.sh` signs with an **Apple Development** certificate if one is in your keychain (a free Apple ID in Xcode → Settings → Accounts → Manage Certificates is enough), so rebuilds don't prompt. Without one it signs ad hoc, and the Keychain asks for your password once per build. `HUDDLE_SIGN_IDENTITY` overrides the choice.

`Scripts/make-icons.sh` redraws `Resources/AppIcon.icns` and `Resources/AppIcon-Dev.icns` from `Scripts/make-icon.swift`.

## Releases

Pushing a tag builds the release app on GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/release.yml`) and publishes it, zipped, as that tag's release in the public releases repository (see Updates): `v1.2.0` becomes version 1.2.0, and a tag with a hyphen (`v1.3.0-beta1`) a pre-release.

A release's notes are its tag's message: tag with `-a`, the first line the release's title, the rest what's new, as paragraphs and `- ` lists. Lines can be broken to fit the terminal (they're joined again), and trailers such as `Co-Authored-By` are left out. The workflow puts them on the release, and in the appcast, where the update window shows them (`Scripts/release-notes.py`, `Scripts/appcast.py`). A tag without a message gets the commits since the last release instead.

```bash
git tag -a v1.4.3 -F notes.txt && git push origin v1.4.3
```

The workflow compiles the Matrix SDK and caches it, so the first run, and the first after the pinned SDK commit changes, takes much longer than the rest.

### Signing and notarization

Releases should be signed with a **Developer ID Application** certificate and notarized, so Huddle opens on any Mac without being allowed first, and macOS recognises it as the same app from one launch and update to the next: the Keychain item with your sessions, and the microphone permission, stay allowed. Without a certificate, releases are signed ad hoc, a new identity every build: Gatekeeper turns them away, and the Keychain asks for access again and again.

1. **The certificate** (needs a paid Apple Developer Program membership; only the account holder can make one): Xcode › Settings › Accounts › your team › Manage Certificates › + › Developer ID Application. Export it with its private key from Keychain Access as a .p12, with a password.
2. **A key for notarization:** App Store Connect › Users and Access › Integrations › App Store Connect API › Team Keys, a new key with the Developer role. Download the .p8 (it can only be downloaded once), and note its Key ID and the Issuer ID shown above the list.
3. **The secrets,** in the release environment (see below): `MACOS_CERTIFICATE_P12`, the base64 of the .p12 (`base64 -i cert.p12 | pbcopy`); `MACOS_CERTIFICATE_PASSWORD`, its password; `NOTARY_API_KEY`, the base64 of the .p8 (`base64 -i AuthKey_XXXXXXXXXX.p8 | pbcopy`); `NOTARY_KEY_ID`; and `NOTARY_ISSUER_ID`.
4. **Try them** before a release: Actions › Release › Run workflow builds, signs and notarizes as a release would, keeps the archive with the run for three days, and publishes nothing. Its Notarize step ends with Gatekeeper's verdict, which should say "Notarized Developer ID".

`Scripts/build-app.sh` signs a release with a Developer ID certificate if one is in the keychain, with the secure timestamp notarization needs, and otherwise, as it does the Dev build, with an Apple Development one (which keeps Keychain access across rebuilds on your own Mac, but which Gatekeeper turns away elsewhere). The certificate is part of how macOS knows the app, so the first build signed differently from the one before asks for Keychain access once; Always Allow settles it.

### Updates

The app finds updates in an appcast, a feed listing each release with its EdDSA signature. The workflow publishes every release, and the appcast, to a repository of releases of their own, `huddle-releases` under the same owner (or the `UPDATES_REPO` repository variable, as `owner/name`). The app reads `appcast.xml` on that repository's `main` branch; `Scripts/build-app.sh` puts that address and the public key in the release app's Info.plist. Pre-releases go in the appcast's beta channel, offered only to those who turn on beta versions.

To set it up:

1. Create the public repository `huddle-releases` (it can be empty).
2. Add two secrets to the release environment (see below): `SPARKLE_PRIVATE_KEY`, the private signing key, and `RELEASES_TOKEN`, a fine-grained personal access token with read and write access to Contents on `huddle-releases` only.

Without them, releases are still built and published here, but not offered as updates. Nor is a build without the `MACOS_CERTIFICATE_P12` secret: signed ad hoc, it would have no hardened runtime, so the workflow stops rather than publish it. The workflow hands the releases token to git only through a credential helper, never in a URL, and checks this repository out without leaving its own token behind.

The signing key is in the login Keychain under the account `huddle`. Sparkle's tools export it again (`.build/artifacts/sparkle/Sparkle/bin/generate_keys --account huddle -x key.txt`) or show its public half (`-p`). Updates signed with any other key are refused, so keep it safe: a lost key means people have to download the next release by hand.

### The release environment

The release job runs in a GitHub environment called `release`, and every secret it uses (the certificate, the notarization key, the Sparkle key and the releases token) is the environment's own, not the repository's. Set up this way, only release tags (and the release branches, for trial runs) get the secrets, and each run waits for your approval: no one who can merely push to the repository, nor a stolen token that can, gets a signed update published.

1. **Settings › Environments › New environment**, named `release`.
2. **Required reviewers:** yourself. Leave "Prevent self-review" off when you're the only one.
3. **Deployment branches and tags › Selected branches and tags:** the tag pattern `v*`, and the branches `main` and `legacy/macos-13` (for trial runs, which need the secrets too).
4. **Environment secrets:** the secrets above, with the same values. GitHub can't show a secret's value again, so bring each from where it lives: the Sparkle key from the Keychain (below), the certificate re-exported, a new releases token (and revoke the old). Then delete them from **Settings › Secrets and variables › Actions**, so nothing outside the environment has them.
5. **Settings › Rules › Rulesets › New tag ruleset:** target the tags `v*`, restrict creations, updates and deletions, with **Repository admin** allowed to bypass, so only you can make or move a release tag.

After a tag is pushed, its run waits under **Actions** with "Review deployments": approve it there (or from the email GitHub sends) and it starts.

## Before distributing

- `AppInfo.clientURI` in `Sources/Huddle/Core/LoginFlow.swift` is the website Huddle reports when registering as an OAuth client. It is a placeholder (`https://huddle.invalid`); replace it with a real site.
- Releases are notarized only once the Developer ID certificate and the notarization key are set up (see Signing and notarization); until then other Macs block the app until it is allowed in System Settings → Privacy & Security.
